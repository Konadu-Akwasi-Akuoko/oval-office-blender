## resolute-desk

- [verified] Overall size: 72 in wide x 48 in deep x 32.5 in high = 1.829 m (E-W) x 1.219 m (N-S) x 0.826 m. These are the figures in the White House curatorial note reproduced in a 1974 memo ('CARVED OAK DESK ... Measurements: height-32 1/2", length-72", width-48"'), and the same numbers Wikipedia carries.
  <sub>https://www.fordlibrarymuseum.gov/library/document/0047/phw19740816-09.pdf (White House curatorial note, Gerald R. Ford Library) and https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Resolute_desk</sub>
- [verified] Weight approx. 1,300 lb = 590 kg.
  <sub>https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Resolute_desk (citing Christie's)</sub>
- [likely] A plinth (base) about 2 in / 0.051 m tall was added under the desk in 1961 for Kennedy, to raise the kneehole. It follows the desk's plan and sits flush with the moulding above it. The original plinth was replaced with a new base in 1986 under Reagan; WHHA says several bases have been fitted and removed over the years. So the Obama-era top height is approx. 0.876 m if 32.5 in is the pre-plinth figure, or 0.826 m if the published height already includes a base.

- [verified] Timber is oak, taken from the hull timbers of HMS Resolute (British Arctic exploration ship, broken up 1879). The White House curatorial record titles the object simply 'CARVED OAK DESK'.
  <sub>https://www.fordlibrarymuseum.gov/library/document/0047/phw19740816-09.pdf ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Resolute_desk</sub>
- [verified] Made 1880 by William Evenden, joiner at Chatham Dockyard, Kent, probably to a design by Morant, Boyd & Blanford of 91 New Bond Street (their stamp is under every exterior drawer front). Lock plates stamped 'BY ROYAL / LETTERS PATENT / FOUR LEVERS / SAFETY LOCK / COMYN CHINC & Co.'
  <sub>https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Resolute_desk (citing Monkman, The White House: Its Historic Furnishings and First Families, p. 294)</sub>
- [verified] Form is a double-pedestal partners desk: two pedestals with a kneehole between, and matching drawer/cupboard arrangements on BOTH long faces. All four vertical faces are carved - the two long faces and the two short ends.
  <sub>https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Resolute_desk ; confirmed visually in https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Barack_Obama_in_the_Oval_Office_view_from_the_west_corridor.jpg (28 Jan 2009)</sub>
- [likely] Each long face carries three frieze drawers under the top: two short ones over the pedestals and one long one over the kneehole - six frieze drawers on the desk. Each pedestal has a lockable cupboard door concealing a letter drawer and a file drawer, giving four cupboard doors in total.

- [likely] Carving inventory on each long face, top to bottom, read off the 2009 and 2015 photographs: (1) ovolo-moulded overhanging top edge; (2) narrow carved bead/dentil band immediately under the top; (3) frieze drawers, each faced with a continuous foliate acanthus scroll band punctuated by circular carved rosette bosses that double as the pulls; (4) each cupboard door is a raised panel with a round-arched head filled with a carved fan/shell and a pendant husk drop below it, and a small rosette-band panel across the bottom of the door; (5) the vertical stiles either side of the kneehole are carved with pendant husk / candelabrum ornament; (6) the kneehole opening has a moulded arched head; (7) the corners of the case are canted at 45 degrees, each canted face carrying a fluted pilaster and a lattice/diaper-carved panel; (8) a carved rosette / paterae band runs along the bottom of each pedestal; (9) a moulded base course, then the added plinth block, which runs continuously across the kneehole.

**Modelling notes:** SCALE AND PLACEMENT
Build to 1 Blender unit = 1 m. Box the desk at X 1.829 (east-west), Y 1.219 (north-south), Z 0.876 (floor to leather top). If you decide the published 32.5 in already includes a base, drop to Z 0.826 - the difference is one plinth block, so model the plinth as a separate object and it becomes a one-value switch.

Place the desk origin at the top-face centre. In a room where +Y is north and the origin is the room centre under the ceiling medallion, set the object at (0, -3.50, 0.876) with zero rotation, long axis on X, the FDR panel face pointing +Y (north, into the room). Rear (south) edge lands at Y = -4.11, about 1.35 m clear of the south wall on the centre line. Note the wall curves away: at the desk's ends (X = +/-0.914) the ellipse is at Y = -5.343, not -5.461, so the corner clearance is about 1.23 m, not 1.35 m. Check the chair fits before you commit.

CONSTRUCTION ORDER
1. Case block 1.829 x 1.219 x 0.753 (from plinth top at 0.085 to underside of top at 0.838). Bevel the four vertical corners at 45 degrees, about 0.08 m off each corner - the canted corners are the single most recognisable silhouette cue and a plain rectangular box will read as a replica, not the real desk.
2. Top slab: separate object, 0.040 thick, plan matched to the case plus a 0.030 overhang all round, canted corners carried through. Ovolo/thumb moulding on the edge - a 2-3 segment bevel is plenty.
3. Plinth: separate block, 0.085 tall, case plan plus 0.05 all round, canted corners carried through. It runs continuously across the kneehole - do not cut it away under the knee recess.
4. Kneehole: boolean a recess 0.60 wide x 0.54 tall into both long faces, arched head, leaving the centre solid between the two pedestals. The 1945 panel is a separate 0.60 x 0.50 plate on the north face only, inset 0.04 behind the pedestal fronts, hinged at the bottom.

WHAT TO MODEL VS WHAT TO FAKE
Model as geometry: the canted corners, the top overhang and its moulding, the plinth step, the frieze/door divisions, the kneehole recess, the arched cupboard door panels as raised panels (a 0.008 m extrusion with a bevel), the fluted pilasters on the canted corners (an array of shallow flutes, cheap and visible in silhouette).

Fake with maps: every scrap of acanthus scroll, husk drop, fan/shell head, rosette band and diaper lattice. These are 0.005-0.010 m deep. Use tiling normal + height maps on the frieze band, the door panel fields, the kneehole stiles and the pedestal bottom bands. Adaptive subdivision plus displacement only if you are rendering a hero close-up.

The one exception is the FDR coat of arms panel. It is deeper relief - roughly 0.025-0.030 m - and it is the thing every viewer looks at. Give it real geometry, or at minimum a high-res displacement with adaptive subdivision. Eagle head turned toward the arrows, i.e. toward the viewer's RIGHT when facing the panel from the room. Getting that backwards is the classic error, because the modern presidential seal faces the other way. Carve '1945' under the shield.

MATERIALS
Oak: base colour #7A5527, roughness about 0.30, a light clearcoat for the waxed sheen. Drive roughness and a slight colour darkening from the same height map you use for the carving so the recesses go to about #3E2711 - the dirt-in-the-crevices reading is what sells old carved oak. Add quarter-sawn oak ray fleck at low contrast on the flat panel fields. Resist the urge to go dark; Obama-era photographs show a warm honey-brown, and in south-window sunlight it reads up to #B98733.

Leather top: base #4A2A18, roughness 0.15-0.20 so it mirrors the windows the way it does in every photograph, fine pebble normal map, and a large-scale mottling mask taking worn areas toward #6B4326. Inset it inside a 0.05-0.06 m oak border. Add the gilt tooling line, #B08A3C, as a thin emissive-free metallic strip about 0.035 m inside the border.

Brass: escutcheons, lock plates and the inscribed plaque at #A8843C, metallic, roughness 0.35. In the Obama era put the plaque on the SOUTH frieze drawer, facing the president - the north drawer is plain carving in every 2009-2015 photo I checked.

LIGHTING CHECK
The desk is backlit by the three south windows in almost every reference. If your render puts the north face in shadow you will lose all the carving, which is exactly what happens in real photographs. That is correct behaviour, not a bug - but if you want the panel readable, add a weak fill from the room side rather than brightening the wood albedo.

**Unknown:** 1. Whether the published 32.5 in / 0.826 m height includes a plinth. The 1961 Kennedy plinth was 2 in, but the 1974 White House curatorial note - written 13 years after the plinth went on - still says 32.5 in. Either the plinth is excluded (true height 0.876 m) or the curatorial figure was copied from an older record. Reagan replaced the base in 1986 and WHHA says several bases have come and gone since, so the exact Obama-era top height is a judgement call. My reading of the 2009 photographs is that the visible base assembly is taller than 2 in - closer to 0.10-0.13 m including the case's own base moulding - which argues for 0.876 m or a little more. Model the plinth separately so this is one number to change.

2. Distance from the south wall. I found no published figure and whitehousehistory.org returns 403, so this is a photo estimate against chair depth and body scale. Confident to about +/- 0.15 m. Also unresolved: whether the desk moved at all in the August 2010 refurbishment. Nothing in the 2010 press coverage says it did, and 2009 and 2014 photographs look identical in placement, so I assumed it did not.

3. Internal drawer arrangement. One source says each pedestal holds a letter drawer and a file drawer behind its cupboard door; another says three drawers per pedestal. Nobody publishes internal dimensions. Irrelevant unless you model the doors open.

4. Whether the two long faces are identical. Photographs of the south (president's) face are rare because someone is always sitting there. I assumed mirror symmetry, which is normal for a partners desk, but the south face has an open kneehole rather than the 1945 panel and carries the brass plaque, so it is not a pure mirror.

5. Secondary timber, drawer linings and the exact plaque dimensions are undocumented in anything I could reach.

6. The desk was refinished in 2025. Any photograph after February 2025 shows a different, lighter surface and should not be used as reference for the 2010-2016 target era.

7. whitehousehistory.org blocked every fetch attempt (403), as warned. Its 'Treasures of the White House' entry and the 'A Resolute Myth' article are the two best sources on the panel and the plinth history, and I could only reach them second-hand through Wikipedia's citations and search summaries. Worth a manual browser visit if you want first-party wording.

## Oval Office (Obama/Michael S. Smith 2010 scheme) — cornice, cove and 1934 ceiling medallion

- [verified] Eric Gugler's 1933–34 scheme gave the room 'a deep bracketed cornice', doors under substantial pediments, bookcases in niches, and 'a ceiling medallion of the Presidential Seal'. There is no ceiling fixture: 'the room is illuminated by light bulbs hidden within the cornice that wash the ceiling in light.'
  <sub>https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oval_Office</sub>
- [verified] Room ellipse: major (N–S) 35 ft 10 in, minor (E–W) 29 ft; ceiling 18 ft 6 in (5.64 m); 'line of rise' (cove spring) 16 ft 7 in (5.05 m). The LONG axis runs north–south (mantel wall to window wall) — confirmed by the ceiling photograph, where the ellipse is taller N–S than wide E–W.
  <sub>https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oval_Office</sub>
- [verified] The cornice is a full Corinthian-type MODILLION cornice, not a dentil cornice. Reading top (ceiling) to bottom (wall): (1) fillet at the cove spring; (2) plain corona/fascia; (3) small bead/astragal; (4) modillion band — a coffered soffit of sunken square panels each holding a circular rosette (patera), separated by fluted bracket blocks that hang below the soffit; (5) egg-and-dart ovolo; (6) a deep plain cavetto/scotia (this is the 'deep' in 'deep bracketed cornice'); (7) a fine running enrichment band (guilloche / bead-and-reel); (8) cyma reversa + fillet die into the wall. NO dentil course exists anywhere in the profile — the 'dentil-looking' ring in low-resolution photos is the modillion/coffer band.
  <sub>https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/6/69/Ceiling_of_the_White_House_Oval_Office_in_its_entirety%2C_including_Portrait_Paintings_and_the_top_of_the_Resolute_Desk_on_September_23%2C_2022.jpg (Official WH photo, Adam Schultz, 2022) cross-checked against https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/e/ec/View_of_Oval_Office_in_2017.jpg</sub>

Key ellipses (semi-axes X = E-W, Y = N-S):
- Wall at floor and at cornice bottom: 4.420 x 5.461, Z 0 to 4.58
- Cornice bottom (wall junction): Z = 4.58
- Coffered modillion soffit ring: approx 4.20 x 5.24, Z approx 4.87

1. CORNICE PROFILE SWEEP. Draw the 8-member profile as a 2D polyline in the XZ plane: total height 0.47, projection 0.30, bottom at Z = 4.58, top at Z = 5.05, and the top edge 0.30 inboard of the wall. Sweep it with a Curve object (Bezier ellipse 4.420 x 5.461) plus a bevel profile curve, or Geometry Nodes Curve to Mesh. Do NOT scale a circle non-uniformly to make the ellipse - that shears the swept profile. Build the ellipse as an actual Bezier ellipse (or Curve Circle then a Geometry Nodes Set Position with the ellipse equation) so the profile stays perpendicular to the curve everywhere.

2. MODILLIONS AND COFFERS - model as real geometry. They read strongly in silhouette in any shot that looks up. Geometry Nodes: Curve Ellipse (4.20 x 5.24) -> Resample Curve by COUNT = 126 (this distributes by arc length automatically, which is what you want on an ellipse) -> Instance on Points -> Rotate Instances to align to the curve tangent with +Z up -> Realize. Instance a single bay unit that contains one fluted bracket (0.08 wide, hanging 0.055 below the soffit, 3-4 flutes) plus one sunken coffer panel (0.135 wide, rosette 0.085 dia). Bay pitch 0.236.

3. EGG-AND-DART - same curve, Resample by COUNT = 252, instance a single egg+dart unit 0.118 wide. At normal viewing distance this can be a normal map on the ovolo instead. Model it only if you plan close-ups.

4. THE FINE GUILLOCHE BAND at the bottom of the cornice - fake it. Normal + roughness map on the swept profile. It is 0.025 m tall and never reads as geometry from the floor.

5. COVE. Loft between the cove-spring ellipse (Z 5.05) and the flat-ceiling ellipse (Z 5.64). Use a quarter-ellipse profile: 0.75 horizontal run, 0.59 rise, vertical tangent at the spring, horizontal tangent at the ceiling. Important: the true offset of an ellipse at constant normal distance is NOT another ellipse. At this scale the error between a true offset curve and a naively shrunk ellipse is only a few millimetres, so shrinking the semi-axes by the run is acceptable - but if you sweep the cove profile along the ellipse normal with Geometry Nodes, the inner edge will come out slightly non-elliptical and that is correct.

6. MEDALLION. Flat relief on the Z = 5.64 plane, no boss, no rim.
   - 50 stars: Geometry Nodes Curve Circle radius 1.03 -> Resample by COUNT = 50 -> Instance an 8-pointed star mesh 0.088 across, extruded 0.010 proud. Rotate each so a point aims radially outward (they are radially aligned in the photo).
   - Incised circle: 1.74 m diameter, a shallow V-groove about 4 mm wide, 2 mm deep. Model or bake.
   - Central roundel 1.19 m diameter: do this as a displacement/normal map, not geometry. Bake or paint a 4k-8k map containing the incised legend (letter height 0.085, clockwise, Roman caps, plus about three florets at the bottom) and the raised eagle group (0.82 wide, max 15 mm relief). Modelling the feathers is wasted effort - the whole thing is 5.6 m overhead and the relief is under 20 mm.
   - Eagle orientation: head turned toward the olive branch. In plan, the seal reads correctly when viewed from below, so lay the texture out as seen from inside the room and do not mirror it.

7. LIGHTING. This is the single thing that makes the room look right or wrong. There are no downlights and no chandelier.
   - Emissive ring: an ellipse strip at Z = 5.06, semi-axes 4.13 x 5.17, about 0.03 tall, normals facing up and inward, hidden behind the cornice top fillet so it is never visible from any camera below 2 m. In Cycles use a mesh emitter (an area light cannot take an elliptical shape). Aim for roughly 3:1 to 4:1 linear luminance between the cove near the spring and the centre of the flat ceiling.
   - Add the Art Moderne sconces flanking the windows and the table lamps, or the room will read as a flat white box.

8. MATERIALS. One material for cornice, cove, ceiling and medallion: base colour approx #F3F0E9, roughness 0.75, no specular tint, no clearcoat. Add a very subtle large-scale bump for hand-run plaster. The cornice reads near #FBFAFA where the cove light hits it directly and around #DBDBDD in the shaded returns - let the lighting produce that, do not paint it in.

WHAT TO FAKE VS MODEL, summary: model the swept cornice profile, the 126 modillions and coffers, the cove, and the 50 stars. Fake the egg-and-dart, the guilloche band, the coffer rosettes (unless close-up), and the entire central seal roundel.

PLACEMENT ON THE ELLIPSE
Use the parametrisation x = 4.420*sin(t), y = -5.461*cos(t), origin at room centre, +y north.
- Centre window: t = 0, at (0.000, -5.461)
- East window: t = +31 deg, at (+2.277, -4.681)
- West window: t = -31 deg, at (-2.277, -4.681)
Critical: rotate each side window by the ELLIPSE NORMAL angle, not by t. At t = 31 deg the outward normal is (0.596, -0.803), i.e. 36.6 deg off due south. Getting this wrong is the single most visible error in Oval Office models - the side windows sit visibly wrong against the desk.
Chord spacing 2.41 between adjacent window centres, so roughly 1.25 of bare wall between window casings.

GLASS - MODEL TWO PLANES
Do not use a single glass plane. Photographs clearly show doubled ghost reflections, which is the giveaway of a historic sash plus a separate interior ballistic panel. Model:
- Outer historic pane in each light, thickness 0.006, set into the muntin rebate.
- A single interior ballistic sheet spanning the whole sash, thickness 0.06 (about 2.5 in), offset 0.04-0.06 behind the muntins, no muntin divisions of its own.
Shader: IOR 1.52, roughness 0.02, a faint green-cyan absorption (transmission tint around #DCEAE2 at 0.06 thickness) and a slightly higher specular than normal glass. Add 1-2 mm of low-frequency noise to the outer pane normal to get the wavy period-glass distortion visible in the photos. The doubled reflection falls out of the two-plane setup automatically - it is a big part of why the room reads as the Oval Office.

MATERIALS - WHAT TO FAKE VS MODEL

LIGHTING CHECK
With no ceiling fixtures and only the cornice cove wash, these three windows are the dominant light source in daylight renders. Because the sill is low (0.50) and the head is high (4.05), the light throw reaches the far north wall. Get the sill height right or the shadow pattern on the floor will be wrong.

**Unknown:** Nothing metric about these windows appears to be published. The White House Historical Association site returns 403 to automated fetching, the Truman Library Oval Office PDF is image-only with no extractable text, and no HABS measured drawings of the 1934 West Wing turned up. Every dimension above is photogrammetric, anchored to the 5.05 m cove spring, the 2.03 m doors and the 1.83 m Resolute Desk, and cross-checked between at least two independent photographs. Treat them as +/-10 percent.

Specific gaps a modeller will have to make a judgement call on:

1. Upper sash row count. The lower sash unambiguously has three rows in every photo. The upper sash is partly hidden behind the valance in every single available image. I read three rows in the near-frontal Nov 2010 shot and assumed 3+3 by the normal symmetry of a double-hung sash. If it were actually a triple-hung window (plausible for a sash this tall in a 1934 building) there would be a third sash hidden entirely behind the valance and the head would sit higher than 4.05 m.

2. Whether there is any entablature above the window heads. Drapery has covered that zone continuously since 1934, so I have never seen it uncovered in any photograph. My conclusion that it is plain architrave with no pediment is inferred from the plain wall visible above the 1934 and Truman pelmets, which sat lower than the current valance.

3. Exact glass thickness and construction. The 2.5 inch figure is a general White House number from a ballistic-glazing vendor, not an Oval Office spec. The Secret Service has never published this. The two-plane construction is inferred from doubled reflections in photographs, which is strong but circumstantial. The 0.04-0.06 m air gap is a guess sized to produce a reflection offset matching the photos.

4. Fabric content and supplier of the red drapes. Michael S. Smith has never published a spec for them as far as I can find. "Woven burgundy" and "bold red" are the only published descriptions. My reading of a tone-on-tone satin stripe in silk or silk-wool comes from magnified photo texture only.

5. Exact colour. There is no published reference. My hex values are white-balance-corrected pixel samples averaged across a daylight photo (Jan 2011) and a tungsten photo (May 2011), using painted white trim and the wallpaper as grey references. The two sources disagree by roughly 10 percent in saturation, and the corrected values skew slightly warm-pink, so I nudged the recommended albedo back toward the raw mid-tone reads.

6. Exact angular positions of the side windows. The +/-31 degrees figure comes from a single pinhole reconstruction of a wide-angle photograph where I had to assume the lens focal length. The 2.4 m centre spacing is better supported because the 1934 photograph gives it independently, so if you have to pick one constraint to honour, honour the 2.4 m spacing and let the angle follow.

7. Precise valance extent. The +/-45 degrees is from the same wide-angle reconstruction and is the least reliable number in the set, since the drape ends sit near the frame edge where my focal-length assumption bites hardest. Anything from +/-42 to +/-50 degrees would look right.

**Modelling notes:** BUILD ONE UNIT, MIRROR IT FOUR TIMES. The arch-and-shell assembly is identical on the east and west walls. Only the fill differs: shelves on the west, window reveals on the east.

Placement. Work in metres with the origin at the ellipse centre, +Y north, +X east, ellipse (x/4.420)^2 + (y/5.461)^2 = 1. Put the four unit centres at (-4.275, +1.389), (-4.275, -1.389), (+4.275, +1.389), (+4.275, -1.389). Rotate each so its inward normal matches the ellipse normal at that point - for the NW niche that is 12.4 deg off due east. Do not just rotate by the bearing angle; on an ellipse the surface normal and the radius are not parallel, and at 18 deg off the minor axis the error is about 6 deg, which is enough to show as a gap at the jambs. Easiest reliable method: build a curve from the ellipse, place empties by arc length (the niche centres are 1.40 m of arc either side of the due-east/due-west point), and use the curve tangent for orientation.

Cut the recess as a boolean into the wall shell, not as a surface detail - the recess is 0.25-0.28 m deep and you will see the jamb returns from almost every camera angle in the room.

Vertical set-out (AFF): dado cap / bookcase base 0.90; bookcase clear opening 1.27 high x 0.90 wide; arch spring 2.17; semicircular arch radius 0.49 over the 0.98 m recess opening; soffit apex 2.66; outer moulding apex 2.72. Overall unit width 1.08 m. Everything above the base moves with the dado cap, so if a sibling agent researching the wainscot returns a different cap height, shift the whole niche rather than re-deriving the parts.

Shelving: four tiers, pitch 0.317 m, three movable boards plus a fixed bottom, boards 22-25 mm with a small ovolo front edge, depth 0.22 m. Set the top tier's ceiling as a flat soffit at the spring line.

Shell head: model as geometry, not a normal map - it is the single most recognisable ornament in the room after the ceiling medallion, it sits at eye-to-camera height in most Oval Office shots, and its silhouette breaks the arch outline where the lobes overlap the plain margin. Recipe: flat lunette tympanum in the wall plane, then a fan of about 21 flutes generated by an array of one lobe around an empty at the umbo (bottom centre of the arch, on the spring line), shell radius 0.44 m so a 0.05 m plain margin is left inside the arch ring. Give the umbo about 50 mm projection and taper to 12 mm of ridge height at the rim. Add a 0.07 m volute scroll at each bottom corner and let the lower lobe group dip about 30-40 mm below the spring line onto the frieze. Subdivide the flute crests - hard-edged flutes read as plastic.

The arch ring itself is a slim moulded archivolt about 40-50 mm wide with a bead - a simple swept profile on a semicircle is fine.

Fake with maps: the tiny bead runs on the shelf edges, the wood grain and gilt tooling on the book spines, and the dado panel mouldings below the niche if they are more than 3 m from camera.

Books: build 3 or 4 instanced spine strips per tier rather than individual volumes. Obama-era palette is navy cloth (#2E3F63) with gilt lettering (#C8A55E) as the dominant note, roughly half a shelf per tier, plus one oxblood set. Leave real gaps - the shelves were deliberately not packed. Interior paint and joinery are the trim off-white (#E9E7E5 in full light); do not run the taupe striped wallpaper into the recess.

Props for the Obama scheme, in rough order of visual payoff: a dark burnished globular Native American jar, a brass-and-wood patent model (the Morse telegraph register is the recognisable one), and a framed document under glass leaning at the front of a lower shelf. Keep the busts out of the niches - MLK and Lincoln belong on the console tables at the fireplace.

**Unknown:** 1. Exact angular position around the ellipse. I could not find any dimensioned plan. whitehousehistory.org is 403, whitehousemuseum.org has lapsed and is now a domain-for-sale page (the Wayback copy has no plan detail), and loc.gov blocked my requests, so HABS-style measured drawings were out of reach. The angles come from calibrating a camera against the 1981 empty-room photograph. The relative offset (1.40 m of arc either side of the side door) is solid, but the absolute position depends on the focal length I fitted, and different landmark choices put the side doors anywhere from 90 to 100 degrees from due south. I assumed the Beaux-Arts intent - side doors exactly on the minor axis - which gives 72 and 108 degrees. If a plan ever turns up and the doors are north of the minor axis, slide all four units north by the same arc.

2. Dado cap height. This is the datum the whole niche hangs from and I could not measure it cleanly - the floor line is occluded by furniture in every wide shot I found, and my two independent estimates (0.78 m from the Trudeau photo scaled off the mantel, 0.90 m from proportion) disagree by 12%. It reads flat in the 1981 detail (even tone across the tympanum, hard shadow line under the shell rim) but the 2017 photograph suggests the scallops may curve back slightly. A judgement call: I would build it flat with the shell in relief, since that matches the east window recesses where the tympanum clearly sits above a rectangular window head.

5. Relief depth. No source, no reliable shadow measurement. The 40-60 mm figure is craft judgement for a plaster shell of this radius.

6. I never found a straight-on, high-resolution photograph of an Obama-era niche. **Modelling notes:** GEOMETRY

Model the rug as a flat elliptical disc, centred on the room centre, semi-axes 3.505 m (X, east-west) and 4.572 m (Y, north-south). Give it a thickness of about 0.015 m with the top face at z = 0.015 (pile ~0.012 m over a ~0.003 m backing) — estimated, no published figure. The edge is a simple serged edge, not a bound tape, so a slightly rounded 3-4 mm bevel is enough.

Do not build the rug by scaling the room ellipse. The room is 1.2355:1 and the rug is 1.3044:1. What is actually constant is the reveal of bare floor: 0.889 m at the north and south ends, 0.915 m at the east and west sides. If you place both ellipses concentrically at their true dimensions this falls out on its own.

BORDER

Four concentric rings, inset from the rug edge:
  0.000 – 0.065 m  navy outer band
  0.065 – 0.255 m  cream lettering band (0.190 m wide)
  0.255 – 0.277 m  gold/olive pinstripe
  0.277 – 0.297 m  navy pinstripe
  inside 0.30 m    open field

Everything here is flat colour. Fake all of it in the texture — no geometry. A single 8K (or 4K plus a separate 8K strip for the border) base-colour map on a UV that runs the ellipse perimeter along U is the cleanest route, because the text has to follow a curve.

TEXT LAYOUT

The text path is the centreline of the cream band, an ellipse with semi-axes 3.345 × 4.412 m; its perimeter is about 24.5 m. Letters are upright when read from OUTSIDE the rug looking in — their tops point toward the rug centre. So on the south edge of the rug the text reads left-to-right as you stand at the desk facing north; on the north edge it will appear upside down from that viewpoint.

Reading direction is counter-clockwise in plan (north up): south → east → north → west.

Set the baseline 0.12 m in from the rug edge with cap height 0.085 m growing inward (cap tops at ~0.205 m in). Small caps 0.065 m. Use a Trajan-style inscriptional serif with roughly 35% extra tracking. The strings are title-cased and rendered as caps + small caps, so "No Problem" comes out as a full-height N and P with small-cap "o" and "roblem".

Arc lengths along the 24.5 m path, allocating ~0.49 m to each of the five star slots and the rest in proportion to character count:
  FDR      3.30 m   centred on due south (bearing 177°)
  MLK      4.88 m   east side, centred ~111° (ESE)
  Lincoln  4.13 m   north-east, centred ~42°
  JFK      3.68 m   north end, centred ~339° (NNW)
  TR       6.08 m   west side, centred ~258° (WSW)

Star bearings from the rug centre (north = fireplace): 148°, 75°, 9°, 309°, 207°. Stars are ~0.10 m across, points-up relative to the text, on the band centreline.

SEAL

A 1.60 m circle at the rug centre. Model nothing — this is a decal/texture job. Build it as a separate high-res texture layer:
  - ivory disc with a ~0.01 m near-black navy outline at the rim
  - ring of 50 small navy stars, centres at radius 0.70 m, each ~0.048 m across, pitch ~0.088 m
  - eagle drawn as a sepia line engraving with cream fill, wingspan ~1.0 m, facing the viewer's left
  - shield 0.35 × 0.45 m, dark slate chief over 13 red/cream vertical stripes
  - E PLURIBUS UNUM scroll, glory of rays and cloud above, olive branch left, 13 arrows right
  - no outer text ring: this is the coat of arms, not the full seal

MATERIAL

One Principled BSDF, roughness ~0.85, specular 0.2, no metallic. Wool takes a lot of light back diffusely; add a weak sheen (0.15-0.25) if your renderer has it. Pile direction matters more than pile height at normal camera distances: drive an anisotropic-looking sheen with a fine stripe map (~5 mm period) running along the rug's long axis, plus a large soft noise (0.3-0.6 m scale) at ~4% value variation for the cloudiness. That "very textural" field is the single most identifiable thing about this rug — get it wrong and it reads as flat felt.

Only use real geometry/hair for the pile if you have a close-up shot planned. For a room-scale render a normal map at ~2 mm bump depth plus the stripe/noise sheen is enough.

WHAT NOT TO DO

No sunburst. No radiating rays in the field. That is the Reagan/Bush rug and it is the single most common mistake in Oval Office models. Any reference photo showing a cream field with rays and a pale-blue seal is 2009 or earlier (or 2017 and later) and is the wrong rug.

**Unknown:** Pinned down only by photogrammetry, not by any published figure — treat as ±10-15%: the seal diameter (1.60 m), all four border band widths, cap height, star size, and the pile height/total thickness. Two independent photographs agree on the border proportions, which is why I trust them more than the seal diameter, which rests on a single perspective-corrected overhead.

The quote-to-room mapping (FDR south, MLK east, Lincoln north-east, JFK north, TR west) is my inference from photographs, not from any source that states it. The chain is: the 31 Aug 2010 AP press-preview frame is shot from the north end looking south, "MORAL UNIVERSE" is legible on the left of that frame and "...LY UPON THE WEL[FARE]" on the right, and facing south puts east on the left. The gold/olive pinstripe colour is the weakest hex in the palette. It is only ~2 cm wide, so in every photograph it is contaminated by the navy on one side and the cream on the other; measurements ranged from #593D22 (shadowed) to #C6BE99 (lit). #9C8548 is a midpoint judgement.

Unrelated to the rug but worth flagging to whoever has the drapes: the 31 Aug 2010 press-preview photographs show gold/olive curtains, while 2011 photographs of the same room show deep red ones. Something changed between those dates and it is not covered by the "2010 redecoration" framing.

WHAT TO MODEL VS WHAT TO FAKE

Model as geometry: the two Ionic columns (fluting included - it catches the cove wash and reads at any camera distance), the shelf profile, the dentil course, the two rosette end blocks, and the raised central tablet as a box. These are all silhouette-critical.

Fake with maps: the carved patera on each end block, the festoon/swag, the bow-knots, the bellflower drops, the guttae, the leaf-and-dart bands, and the egg-and-dart on the capitals. Use a normal + height map on the tablet face and block faces. The relief is genuinely shallow - under 8 mm - so displacement is wasted unless the camera comes within a metre.

If the camera does get close, the tablet carving in order: 0.076 dia rosette at top centre; a laurel/husk garland sagging about 60 percent of the tablet height, tied at the bottom centre with a crossed X ribbon; a ribbon bow-knot at each end above a tapering husk drop; five small tapered guttae under each drop.

MATERIAL

The width-to-height ratio of 1.50 and the opening ratio of 1.40 are reliable.

The marble variety is not documented anywhere I could find. Sources say only 'white marble'. Vermont white and Italian Carrara statuario are both plausible for a 1909 Washington government commission; Vermont is the likelier procurement given the period and the federal preference for domestic stone, but that is inference, not evidence. The grey-green cast in the veining is real and visible in photographs.

whitehousehistory.org returns 403 to WebFetch, as warned, and it is the one body most likely to hold an actual measured description or a Nathan Wyeth drawing. HABS DC-37 at the Library of Congress covers the Residence and has 41 measured drawings, but I did not find a West Wing mantel elevation among them - worth a targeted search of loc.gov if exact numbers become important.

Depth is the weakest figure. It comes from one oblique photograph with an estimated viewing angle, so 0.22 m could reasonably be anywhere from 0.18 to 0.28. Hearth depth is worse - 0.45 m is derived from a perspective calculation that needed an assumed camera height, and it could be anywhere from 0.36 to 0.55.

The identity of the mantel plant during the Obama years is genuinely ambiguous. Wikipedia records that the Swedish ivy was temporarily swapped for grape ivy at some point in that administration, and the leaf shape in the October 2013 photographs is toothed and rhomboid, which reads as grape ivy rather than Swedish ivy. I could not establish when the swap happened or when it was reversed, so a model set in a specific year is a judgement call. Four pots is the count in the 2013 photograph; other years may differ, since the article notes presidents varied between one pot and several.

The portrait attribution needs a caveat. Wikipedia and press coverage call it the Rembrandt Peale 'porthole' portrait, but the standard porthole shows Washington in civilian black, and the Oval Office painting clearly shows him in Continental Army uniform with epaulettes. It is a military-dress variant of the porthole type. The Bush White House archive separately identifies a Charles Willson Peale Washington over the mantel in the Carter era, which is a different and non-oval painting - so the picture above the mantel has changed between administrations even though the mantel has not. For 2010-2016 the oval uniformed version is the correct one, confirmed in photographs from 2011, 2013 and January 2017.

WALL-HUGGING PIECES
For anything against the curved wall (chest, photo table, consoles, side chairs, clock) the back should follow the ellipse tangent, not sit square. Snap them with a short script: pick a wall parameter t, wall point P = (4.420*sin t, 5.461*cos t), outward normal N = normalize((P.x/4.420^2, P.y/5.461^2)), then object origin = P - N*(depth/2) and rot_z = atan2(-N.x, -N.y). That is exactly how the azimuths in the table above were derived, so it will reproduce them.

WHAT TO MODEL AS GEOMETRY
- Sofas. They are the signature object and appear in every shot. Model the roll arms as real geometry (a swept 0.22 m roll that flares outward and runs down the arm face), three loose boxed seat cushions and three loose back cushions as separate objects so you can slump them slightly, and a flat tailored skirt to the floor with a 0.03 m braid strip at the hem. Give the cushions a light Cloth sim pass or a Simple Deform + shrinkwrap so they do not read as boxes. Do NOT add nailheads.
- Coffee table. Trivial geometry: a 1.40 x 0.90 x 0.44 block. Its whole character is the tile grid, so do that in the shader, not with 400 separate tile meshes.
- Armchairs and the wooden side chairs. These need real carved legs and arm supports; a box will not pass. If time is short, buy or reuse a Chippendale lolling chair and a Louis XVI caned chair and re-skin them.
- Resolute desk. Only the front and the two visible ends matter for most camera angles, but the carved front panel with the presidential seal is heavily photographed. Model the plinth as a separate 0.05 m block you can toggle.
- Flagpoles. Model the pole, base and eagle finial; make each flag a subdivided plane with a Cloth sim pinned along the hoist, settled into a soft vertical spiral, then apply. Gold fringe as a thin strip of alpha-cut geometry along three edges.

WHAT TO FAKE WITH MAPS
- Coffee table mica: a 0.11-0.13 m square grid in base colour + roughness + a shallow bump. Vary tile albedo randomly between #8B7361 and #AE998D so the grid does not tile visibly. Roughness around 0.45, slight anisotropy.
- Sofa velvet: base colour #9C8A66, a velvet/sheen BSDF or Principled with Sheen 0.6-0.8 and Sheen Tint toward #C6B79B. Add a fine woven-grid normal map at roughly 2 mm pitch. Pile response is what sells it: the arm rolls should blow out toward #C2B198 at grazing angles and the skirt should sink to #5A462C.
- Rug: one texture. Wheat field #D5C6AE, the presidential seal medallion at the centre (about 1.7 m across, centred exactly on the origin), the quotation ring around the border. Give it a shell-fur or a low-height displacement so the edge is not a knife.
- Leather on the armchairs: #8C5A34 base with a coarse pebble normal, roughness 0.4, and darkened creases at the seat and inside back. Brass nailheads along the arm and seat edges can be a normal + metallic mask rather than geometry.

LIGHTING CONSEQUENCE OF THIS LAYOUT
There are no ceiling fixtures. Only the two table lamps at (±1.75, +3.95) with their shade tops at 1.54 m, plus the concealed cornice wash. That means the seating group is lit from its two north corners and everything south of about y = 0 is lit by daylight from the three windows. Sun through the south windows will throw the window mullion shadows across the rug and up the west sofa in the morning and the east sofa in the afternoon. Set the lamp bulbs around 2700 K and the cove around 3000 K; the daylight is the only cool source.

**Modelling notes:** GEOMETRY BUDGET. Model two door types, not four. Type A (pedimented) is used twice, at the east and west ends of the minor axis. Type B (jib) is used twice, mirrored about the N-S axis.

PLACEMENT. Put the ellipse centre at the world origin, +X east, +Y north, +Z up. Semi-axes: X 4.420, Y 5.461.
- East door case: centre (4.420, 0.000), face normal +X.
- West door case: centre (-4.420, 0.000), face normal -X.
- NE jib door: leaf centre (2.870, 4.150), outward normal bearing 46.6 deg east of north, i.e. normal direction (0.726, 0.688, 0). Do NOT use the radial direction (34.7 deg) — on an ellipse the normal and the radius differ by 12 deg here, and using the radius will visibly skew the leaf against the wall.
- NW jib door: mirror to (-2.870, 4.150), normal (-0.726, 0.688, 0).

TYPE A — PEDIMENTED CASE (build once, mirror). All heights from finished floor.
Clear opening 1.10 x 2.40. Head at 2.40.
Architrave: outer edges 1.81 apart, so 0.355 per side. Model as a flat frame 0.26 wide with a 0.11 plain margin outside it. Give it crossettes: at the head the frame steps outward by about 0.06 on each side. Project the whole architrave 0.05-0.06 from the wall face.
Bead-and-reel band across the head: 2.55 to 2.60.
Pulvinated frieze: 2.60 to 2.82, width 1.86 between the round terminal bosses. Model as a half-cylinder of radius 0.11 lying horizontally, projecting 0.10 from the wall. Cut 9 or 10 shallow flutes along its length (reeds). Wrap four flat straps around it. At the exact centre lay two flat ribbons about 0.05 wide crossing in an X at roughly 45 deg. Cap each end with a sphere-ish boss of radius 0.11 carrying a rosette.
Egg-and-dart bed mould: 2.86 to 2.96, running the full 2.15 width. Egg pitch 0.070, band depth 0.075. Make ONE egg-and-dart unit and array it — you need it here, and again up both rakes.
Horizontal cornice: top at 3.07, overall width 2.15, projecting about 0.22-0.25 from the wall.
Pediment: tympanum triangle 1.63 base x 0.33 rise sitting on the cornice, i.e. apex of the tympanum at 3.40. Pitch 22 deg (set rise/run = 0.404). Outer profile of the raking cornice puts the apex at 3.60-3.75. Raking cornice section, from the tympanum outward: egg-and-dart ovolo (same unit, rotated 22 deg and arrayed along the rake), fillet, bead-and-reel/rope, plain fascia, outer cyma. Mitre everything at the apex. Tympanum field is flat and plain — no ornament.
The case is FLAT. Because the wall curves at R=6.75 there, either recess the wall 86 mm behind the case centre or let the case stand proud; do not try to bend it.

TYPE A LEAVES.
East: a pair, each 0.55 x 2.27, fully glazed with true divided lights, 2 wide x 4 high, muntins about 22 mm. Hinged on the outer stiles, three hinges each, opening inward. Lock rail at 1.05 with two brass lever handles back to back on the meeting stiles, plus bolts at the foot. Set the pair back in a deep panelled reveal — the east reveal is a good 0.5-0.7 deep with a tall recessed reeded panel in each jamb.
West: one leaf, 1.03 x 2.25, six raised-and-fielded panels (two small square at top, two tall at centre, two rectangular at bottom). Hinged on the NORTH jamb, three hinges, knob on the south stile at 0.95, opens inward. Shallow reveal, about 0.15-0.20.

TYPE B — JIB DOOR. This is the cheap one and it matters that it stays cheap-looking. A plain flat slab 1.13 x 2.24, flush with the wall surface, no casing, no architrave, no pediment, no panel mouldings on the Oval Office face. Give it a 3 mm reveal gap on all four sides — that hairline is the ONLY thing that reads. One small round brass knob, diameter about 0.055, on the lock edge at 1.00. The dado rail and the wainscot panelling must terminate cleanly at the vertical joints and NOT continue across the leaf. Opens inward. If you need the outer face for an open-door shot, make it a six-panel door on that side.

WHAT TO MODEL VS WHAT TO FAKE.
Model as geometry: the whole pedimented case profile (extrude a swept section), the pulvinated frieze cylinder with its straps and the central X, the crossettes, the door leaves and their panels, the glazing bars. These all silhouette against a bright window wall and read in every hero angle.
Fake with maps: the egg-and-dart and bead-and-reel enrichment — normal plus a light height/displacement map on a simple ovolo is enough at any realistic camera distance, and it saves you from arraying roughly 25 eggs on the cornice plus another 40-odd up the two rakes on two doorcases. Same for the running leaf band on the architrave edge (0.08 wide, low relief) and the foliate bosses on the frieze ends. If you do displace, keep it under 6 mm.
Material: one warm off-white enamel for every bit of the door joinery, base colour about #F4F3EF, roughness 0.30-0.40, semi-gloss. The trim is only marginally lighter than the 2010 striped wallcovering, so do not push it to pure white or the room will look wrong.

LIGHTING NOTE. There are no ceiling fixtures; the cornice washes the cove. That means the pediments are lit from ABOVE and slightly behind, so the raking cornice throws a hard shadow onto the tympanum and the egg-and-dart under the horizontal cornice sits in shade. Get that shadow right and the ornament reads even if the maps are coarse.

**Modelling notes:** WALLPAPER
Build it as a procedural or baked texture, not geometry. Stripe repeat 0.1524 m, two equal 0.0762 m stripes. Base colours: buff #DCD2BD, cafe-au-lait #D5C4A9. The contrast is deliberately tiny (about 6 percent luminance) — resist the urge to push it, or the room stops looking like the photographs.

UV setup: the papered field wraps a true ellipse with a perimeter of 31.14 m (Ramanujan approximation on a = 5.461, b = 4.420). That is 204 full stripe repeats, about 408 stripes, before you subtract the door, window and niche openings. Unwrap the wall as a single strip with U mapped to arc length so the stripe width stays constant around the curve — do NOT project along an axis or the stripes will fan out at the ends of the long axis.

Vertical extent: the paper runs from the top of the dado cap at 0.70 m up to the underside of the cornice. The cove springs at 5.05 m; with a deep bracketed cornice of roughly 0.35-0.45 m the paper top lands near 4.6 m, giving a field about 3.9 m tall. Confirm the cornice depth against whoever is researching the cornice before you commit that number.

Hand-painted character: add a low-amplitude vertical streak (a stretched noise, scale about 30:1 vertically) inside the darker stripe only, plus a very slight horizontal wobble on the stripe edge — amplitude around 1-2 mm over a 1 m run. Keep the edges soft, roughly a 1.5 mm gradient, not a hard step. Elizabeth Dow builds these in at least three paint layers, so a faint mottle in the base is right.

Sheen: the matte/satin alternation is unconfirmed and the photographs do not support it. Use one roughness across both stripes, around 0.85 (flat/eggshell paint on paper). If you want to hedge, give the darker stripe 0.80 and the lighter 0.88 — that is at the limit of what will ever show and it will not break the look if the claim turns out to be wrong.

Paper is hung in vertical drops. On a wall this curved the drops have to be narrow to lie flat — 0.5 to 0.7 m is realistic. If you are chasing that level of detail, add a barely visible vertical seam every 0.61 m (a 27-inch roll would give exactly 9 stripes per drop, which is a tidy assumption but unverified).

DADO / WAINSCOT
Total height 0.70 m from finished floor to top of cap. Section from the top down: cap moulding 0.06 m (small ogee plus fillet, projecting about 20 mm from the panel face), plain upper rail 0.09 m, recessed panel opening 0.35 m, lower rail plus plinth 0.20 m. Panel recess 10-15 mm behind a quirked ovolo about 15 mm wide.

Panels are long horizontal rectangles, 0.9-1.6 m wide, with 0.10-0.13 m stiles. Do not build a repeating array. Lay the panels out per wall segment: measure each run between architraves and divide it into whole panels, so stiles always land against the door and window casings. Drop in a short 0.5 m panel where a segment will not divide evenly. Model it as a swept profile along the ellipse with panel recesses boolean'd or inset — a swept profile alone will not give you the panel divisions.

The dado does not run behind the south windows; it returns into the window architraves as an apron below each sill. The two arched niches land directly on the cap — set the niche bottom shelf at 0.70 m.

Paint: single off-white #EFECE5 for the dado, cornice, all architraves and pediments, the niche shells and the baseboard. Roughness 0.5-0.6 (a satin trim paint), a touch glossier than the wallpaper. That sheen difference between trim and paper is more visible in the photographs than any stripe sheen difference, so it is worth getting right.

FLOOR
Model the field as real geometry, not a texture — the mitres and the board joints are large enough to read, and a flat texture will not sell the gloss reflections.

Boards 0.1016 m wide, 0.019 m thick, rift and quartered. Alternate one white oak, one walnut — an 0.2032 m two-tone repeat measured across the boards.

Layout: draw the ellipse (a = 5.461, b = 4.420). Inset a 0.3556 m border following the curve; split it 0.1016 walnut / 0.1524 oak / 0.1016 walnut, outer strip to inner strip. Inside the border, cut the remaining area into four sectors with the major and minor axes. Fill each sector with boards at 45 degrees to the axes, and mirror the board direction between adjacent sectors so every board meets its neighbour in a mitre across an axis. The result is chevrons pointing outward along both axes and a cross reading from the room centre — that is what the bare-room photographs show.

Practical approach: build one quadrant's board array flat, boolean it to the quadrant triangle, then mirror it twice. Keep a 0.5-1 mm gap or a bevel on each board edge so the joints catch light; without it the floor renders as a printed pattern.

Materials: oak base colour #B98A4E with visible ray fleck (quarter-sawn white oak — add the fleck as a texture, it is characteristic and worth the effort); walnut base #5B4B38, desaturated with a slight olive cast, much plainer grain. Both under a single gloss coat: roughness 0.10-0.15, clearcoat on, IOR 1.5. The photographs show the windows mirrored in the floor, so push the specular rather than the diffuse.

Fake with maps rather than model: the ray fleck and pore texture, the wallpaper stripes and brush striation, the fine ogee profiles on the panel mouldings if you are far from the camera. Model as geometry: the dado cap and panel recesses, the board joints and mitres, the floor border. Everything at 0.70 m and below gets seen close up in any eye-level camera.

**Unknown:** 1. The matte/satin stripe alternation. No source states it, and my photometry argues against a strong version of it — the stripe contrast holds at 5-8 percent across five wall angles in two photographs and never inverts, which is pigment behaviour, not sheen behaviour. The Washington Post's two colour names ("cafe-au-lait-and-buff") already explain everything visible. Judgement call: model a single roughness. If the parent agent has a source I could not reach (Michael S. Smith's 2020 book "Designing History" is the likely one, and it is not online), it should override this.

2. Whether "three-inch-wide stripes" means each stripe is 3 in or the repeat is 3 in. I read it as each stripe, giving a 6 in repeat, and the photogrammetry is consistent with that — but only if the door opening is about 2.27 m. If the door really is 2.03 m as the brief states, the measured stripe comes out at 2.7 in per stripe, and the whole scale ladder shifts. This is the single biggest unresolved cross-check in the topic and it also moves the dado height.

3. Exact hex values. All colour figures are photographic estimates, white-balanced against auto-detected neutral whites in each frame. The room is lit by tungsten cove uplighting mixed with south daylight, and the three source photographs disagree with each other by roughly 3-5 percent per channel even after correction. Real paint chips will read a little deeper than these values — if the render looks washed out, drop both stripe colours about 5 percent to around #D6CBB4 and #CDBB9D. No paint or wallcovering specification for this job has ever been published.

4. Dado height. 0.70 m is derived, not documented. Range 0.63-0.76 m depending on the door assumption. No measured drawing of the Oval Office is publicly available. HABS documentation of the 1934 West Wing may exist at the Library of Congress but I could not locate it.

5. Dado panel widths. The layout is bespoke per wall segment and I could only measure a couple of runs, at an angle, in perspective. The 0.9-1.6 m figure is a reasonable working range, not a measurement. A modeller will have to eyeball the divisions against reference photographs for each segment.

6. Whether the floor field alternates strictly 1:1. The scans read as equal-width bands, and a single 4-inch board width for both species makes 1:1 the simplest reading, but I occasionally saw what might be a faint sub-division inside a light band. If it turns out to be 2 oak : 1 walnut in places, the repeat becomes 12 in rather than 8 in.

7. Floor stain colours. "Light gold on the oak, grayish-green on the walnut" comes from a museum blog, not a primary source. The floor is glossy enough that pixel sampling is badly contaminated by reflections, so my hex values there are the weakest numbers in this report.

8. Access problems worth noting for anyone following up: whitehousehistory.org returns 403 as warned; woodfloorbusiness.com is behind Cloudflare (readable through web.archive.org); whitehousemuseum.org has been taken down and the domain is for sale, but the 2016 Wayback capture is intact and is the source of the two most useful images in this research — the 2005 bare-room flooring photograph and the 2014 empty-room photograph. Both are worth pulling down and keeping as modelling reference; they are the only clear views of the floor and the dado without furniture or a rug.

**Modelling notes:** GEOMETRY AND PLACEMENT

Set up a helper empty at the room centre and place every wall-mounted item with a single driver: for a bearing theta (0 deg = due north / mantel centre, positive clockwise toward east), the wall point is x = 4.420*sin(theta), y = 5.461*cos(theta). Rotate each frame so its -Y faces the room centre. Because the wall is a true ellipse, the surface normal is NOT radial - use the ellipse normal (nx, ny) proportional to (sin(theta)/4.420, cos(theta)/5.461), normalised. For paintings under 1.3 m wide the difference is under 3 degrees, so a radial approximation is fine; for the 1.49 m wide upper Hopper it is visible, so use the true normal there.

WHAT TO MODEL VS WHAT TO FAKE

Model as geometry:
- Picture frames. Build one parametric gilt frame: a bevelled outer moulding 0.085-0.11 m wide, 0.06 m deep, with a 0.012 m fillet at the rabbet. Duplicate and scale per painting rather than modelling seven frames. The Peale Washington is the exception - it needs an ornate carved crest and should be a separate mesh.
- The two console/card tables flanking the fireplace, the SW cabinet and the SE bow-front chest. These read clearly in every photo and carry the sculptures, so they cannot be faked.
- The Bronco Buster silhouette. A cowboy on a rearing horse at 0.57 x 0.46 x 0.22 m is the single most recognisable object in the room after the desk. If you cannot sculpt it, use a low-poly proxy plus a strong dark metal shader - the silhouette matters more than the detail.
- The two busts as simple head-and-shoulders forms on plinths. MLK 0.43 m total, Lincoln 0.50 m total.
- The tall-case clock: a 2.69 m box with a broken-arch hood, three brass finials, a glazed trunk door and a painted dial. Worth real geometry because it breaks the wall line in every north-east view.
- Niche shelves: four white shelves per niche, roughly 0.28 m apart, 0.24 m deep.

Fake with maps:
- All painting canvases: a single quad with an emissive-free image texture, plus a very slight roughness variation map to suggest impasto. Do not try to model brush texture.
- Book sets on the niche shelves: one modelled block of spines per shelf run, textured. At 0.28 m shelf pitch a diffuse + normal map is enough.
- The Swedish ivy on the mantel: an alpha-mapped card cluster, or a single instanced ivy pot duplicated eight times along the shelf. Real leaf geometry is not worth it here.
- Family photographs behind the desk: textured quads in thin frame extrusions.
- Native American pottery: model as lathe/spin profiles (five minutes each) and put the painted designs entirely in the texture. Martinez blackware needs a low-roughness black shader with a matte-on-polished pattern in the roughness map, not geometry.
- Patent models: too small to read at any normal camera distance. Low-poly brass-and-wood blocks with a metal shader.

TEXTURE SOURCING
Hassam, Moran, Peale Washington, Story Lincoln and Bronco Buster all have clean public-domain files on Wikimedia Commons (URLs in the findings). The Rockwell and both Hoppers are still in copyright, so there is no free flat scan - crop the canvases out of the official White House photographs listed in the findings, then perspective-correct them in Blender's compositor or an image editor before using them as textures.

ERA GATING
If you want a single canonical state, model the LATE configuration (Feb 2014 to Jan 2017): Rockwell on the north wall west of the mantel, Hassam on the south-west wall above the Bronco Buster, Hoppers stacked on the south-east wall. It is the best documented and the most photographed. If you want the early state (2010-2013), swap three objects only: put the Rockwell back on the south-west wall, the Hassam on the south-east wall, and a plain black-framed document (approx 0.55 x 0.45 m, wide white mat) on the north wall west of the mantel. Everything else is identical, so a pair of collections toggled by a boolean is enough.

A useful sanity check while blocking out: the Seymour clock is 2.69 m and verified. Use it, not the doors, to calibrate wall heights in any photo you are tracing.

**Unknown:** 1. Exact framed dimensions. Only the CANVAS sizes are documented (Hassam 1.067 x 0.565 m, Rockwell 0.546 x 0.430 m, Moran 0.775 x 0.524 m, Cobb's Barns 0.872 x 1.272 m, Burly Cobb's House 0.641 x 0.921 m). Every FRAMED figure I give is my own estimate, derived by adding a measured frame width read off the photographs. Expect plus or minus 0.08 m on each dimension.

2. The White House Rembrandt Peale Washington. Its canvas size is not published anywhere I could reach. Rembrandt Peale painted roughly eighty porthole versions in several sizes. I used the common 36 x 29 in as the basis, but when I scaled the frame against the Moran in the Pope Francis photograph the Washington came out noticeably smaller than that implies. It may well be a 29 x 24 in version. Treat my 1.15 x 0.95 m framed figure as a working guess and check it against a photograph before committing. whitehousehistory.org, which would settle this, returns HTTP 403 to WebFetch, and obama.artifacts.archives.gov returns 403 to both WebFetch and curl.

3. The Saint-Gaudens Lincoln bust size. The White House Historical Association page is behind the same 403. A comparable Saint-Gaudens Lincoln bust at the Brooklyn Museum is 0.71 x 0.43 x 0.36 m, but the Oval Office example reads distinctly smaller in photographs. My 0.50 m total height is a photo estimate.

4. The framed document on the north wall west of the mantel between mid-2010 and late 2013. The Emancipation Proclamation copy is documented for a six-month loan beginning 18 January 2010, so whatever hangs in the October 2013 Malala photograph is something else. It is portrait format, black frame, wide white mat, dense text. Candidates are a Gettysburg Address facsimile or the framed 1963 March on Washington programme, but I could not confirm which. If you model the early configuration you will have to make a judgement call on its content.

5. Which of the two west niches held which pottery. The photographs show pots in both niches at different dates, and the shelf arrangement was clearly rotated over the years. I would not treat any single shelf layout as canonical - pick one dated photograph and copy it.

6. Bronco Buster cast identity. The claim that the Oval Office example is cast no. 23 at 22.5 in comes from a commercial reproduction seller, not a museum or White House source. The overall size is consistent with the photographs, but do not repeat the cast number as fact.

7. Exact bearings. Every theta value in these findings is read off photographs, not from a plan. They are good to roughly plus or minus 4 degrees, which at the wall is about plus or minus 0.35 m of arc. If precise placement matters, trace the Pope Francis and Kerry photographs against your ellipse rather than trusting my angles."
